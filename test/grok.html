Jenkins argues that we should separate romantic love from happiness. Culture treats happiness as a condition of love, through ideas like “happily ever after,” so sadness inside a relationship gets read as failure. She denies that. Parents can be exhausted and sad without that meaning they do not love their children, and romantic love should be allowed the same range. What matters is meaningfulness, and love as something we do with an autonomous person, not a prize whose job is to make us happy. Sadness can still be a reason to leave. It is just not, by itself, proof that the love was fake.
